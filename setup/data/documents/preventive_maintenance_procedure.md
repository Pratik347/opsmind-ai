# Preventive Maintenance Schedule and Procedures

**Document ID:** SOP-PM-003
**Revision:** 4.0
**Effective Date:** 2026-07-01
**Applicable Equipment:** All manufacturing equipment
**Applicable Components:** All

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the preventive maintenance (PM) schedule and procedures for all manufacturing equipment across Midwest Manufacturing (PLT-001) facilities. Adherence to this schedule is mandatory and deviations must be documented and approved by the Maintenance Manager.

## 2. Maintenance Philosophy

Our preventive maintenance program follows a tiered approach:

1. **Time-based PM** — Scheduled at fixed intervals based on calendar time or operating hours.
2. **Condition-based PM** — Triggered by monitored parameter trends (vibration, temperature, oil analysis).
3. **Predictive maintenance** — Uses trend analysis and AI-driven anomaly detection to anticipate failures before they occur.

All three tiers complement each other. Time-based PM ensures baseline coverage; condition-based PM catches issues between scheduled intervals; predictive maintenance provides early warning for complex failure modes.

## 3. CNC Milling Center Maintenance Schedule

### 3.1 Daily Checks (Operator Responsibility)

- Coolant level and condition
- Chip removal and enclosure cleanliness
- Unusual noise or vibration during operation
- Way cover and seal integrity
- Tool holder seating

### 3.2 Weekly Checks (Maintenance Technician)

- Lubrication system oil level and flow verification
- Coolant concentration and pH test
- Air filter condition
- Way and guideway cleanliness

### 3.3 Monthly Checks

- Spindle runout measurement
- Axis backlash check
- Hydraulic/pneumatic pressure verification
- Electrical cabinet inspection (visual)

### 3.4 Quarterly Checks

- Coolant system flush and filter replacement
- Axis alignment verification
- Ball screw inspection and preload check

### 3.5 Semi-Annual Checks

- **Bearing inspection and lubrication** — Inspect all spindle and support bearings per SOP-BRG-001. This is the most critical semi-annual task for CNC milling equipment. Failure to complete bearing inspections on schedule has historically been the leading cause of unplanned bearing failures.
- Spindle motor insulation resistance test
- Complete lubrication system service
- Geometric accuracy verification

### 3.6 Annual Checks

- Comprehensive spindle assembly inspection
- Bearing replacement assessment (replace if approaching 18-month service life or if condition monitoring indicates wear)
- Full geometric accuracy certification
- Electrical system thermal imaging survey
- Safety system function test

## 4. CNC Lathe Maintenance Schedule

Similar to CNC Milling Center with the following differences:

- Turret indexing accuracy check: monthly
- Chuck jaw condition: weekly
- Tailstock alignment: quarterly
- Spindle bearing inspection: every 6 months (same as CNC Mill)

## 5. Hydraulic Press Maintenance Schedule

### Key Intervals

- Hydraulic fluid analysis: monthly
- Seal inspection: quarterly
- Hydraulic pump bearing inspection: every 6 months
- Full hydraulic system overhaul: every 24 months

## 6. Assembly Robot Maintenance Schedule

### Key Intervals

- Joint calibration verification: monthly
- Gear reducer oil analysis: quarterly
- Joint bearing lubrication: every 6 months
- Full joint overhaul: every 36 months

## 7. Overdue Maintenance Policy

Maintenance tasks that are not completed within 7 calendar days of their scheduled date are classified as **overdue** and trigger the following escalation:

| Days Overdue | Escalation Level |
|---|---|
| 1–7 days | Maintenance Supervisor notified; reason documented |
| 8–14 days | Maintenance Manager notified; risk assessment required |
| 15–30 days | Plant Manager notified; formal deviation report |
| >30 days | Machine placed on restricted operation pending completion |

**Note:** Overdue bearing inspections are a known risk factor for unplanned failures. Equipment with overdue bearing inspections operating with elevated vibration or temperature should be treated as priority maintenance regardless of the overdue duration.

## 8. Maintenance Documentation

All maintenance activities must be documented in the maintenance management system within 24 hours of completion, including:

- Work performed and components serviced
- Parts replaced (with part numbers)
- Measurements taken (vibration, temperature, alignment)
- Technician name and time spent
- Next scheduled maintenance date
- Any anomalies or concerns noted during the work

## 9. Spare Parts Policy

Critical spare parts, including bearings for all CNC spindles, must be maintained in stock. Current minimum stock levels:

| Part | Minimum Quantity |
|---|---|
| CNC Milling spindle bearing set (angular contact) | 2 sets |
| CNC Lathe spindle bearing set | 2 sets |
| Hydraulic press seal kit | 3 kits |
| Coolant pump motor | 1 unit per machine type |
